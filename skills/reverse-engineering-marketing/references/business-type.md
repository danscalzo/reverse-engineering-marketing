# Business type first: it decides what "well built" means

Identify the type from the site and offer (OBSERVED), then check the points for that type. A structure that is right for one type is wrong for another.

| Type | Signs | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| **Ecommerce** | cart, checkout, product pages, prices | Feed / Merchant Center quality, Shopping or PMax with listing groups, purchase event with **value**, tROAS, product page vs category landing, catalogue / dynamic ads on Meta, abandoned-cart retargeting, AOV and margin (UNKNOWN unless given). |
| **Lead gen, local service** | form, call button, service area | Geo radius vs real catchment, search intent keywords, call assets and call tracking, lead form or landing form length, thank-you page conversion, speed to lead, offline conversion import (lead -> sale). |
| **High-ticket / regulated** (property, loans, insurance, vehicles) | rates, "check eligibility", regulator notes | Special ad category limits on Meta, pre-qualification form, catalogue-driven ads (one product = one ad), compliance text, approvals vs leads. |
| **Bookings / hospitality / events** | calendar, booking engine | Booking engine tracking (cross-domain), dates and seasonality, event deadlines, retargeting of date searchers. |
| **Personal brand / info products** | content, courses, coaching | Content -> lead magnet -> email -> offer ladder, webinar or VSL, ads on top content. See `personal-brand.md`. |
| **B2B** | demo, quote, case studies | Long cycle, CRM stages imported as conversions, LinkedIn, account lists, content offers. |
| **App** | store links | Install vs in-app events, app campaigns. |

## Local service vs ecommerce: two different funnels
- **Ecommerce**: identify the platform first (Shopify, WooCommerce...: tag-scan third-party hosts, page source). Then judge product pages, checkout steps, payment options, feed and purchase tracking with value. A mainstream platform gives a sound default checkout: the gaps are usually in feed, offer and tracking, not in the cart.
- **Local service**: everything depends on how the site is built. Check geo-optimisation (town and area in titles, pages per area, Google Business Profile linked), SEO basics, visibility in AI answers (GEO/AEO: ask the main AI assistants "best <service> in <town>" and record who is named), tracking, copy, and whether the page is built to convert (one action, proof, short form, call button).

## Which channel fits (search vs social)
- **Google first** when people actively search for the product or service because they need it (urgent or planned need, clear keywords). Check: search volume in that country and language (Keyword Planner, Trends).
- **Meta first** when the product has to stop the scroll: visual, impulse, new or unknown category, demand that has to be created.
- **Both** when there is search demand AND the product shows well visually; Meta then also feeds branded search.
- Judge the subject against this: a business with strong search demand and no Google presence, or a visual impulse product only on Search, is a gap (INFERRED until numbers say otherwise).

## Questions to answer for any type
- What is the money event (sale, approved application, booking, signed contract) and how far is it from what the platforms optimise for?
- Is the funnel one step (buy now) or many (lead -> call -> visit -> sale)?
- Which channel matches the intent: search (people already looking) or social (create demand)?
