# Organic communication (not only ads)

Why it matters: people who see an ad often check the profile before trusting. Organic is the credibility layer that makes ads work (or fail). Ads distribute, organic convinces. Example: a brand runs ad-only posts while its Instagram profile stays a separate showcase.

## What to look at (for each live channel: IG, FB, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn, Google Business, newsletter, blog)
1. **Presence**: which channels exist, which are alive (last post, date), where they are strongest.
2. **Cadence**: posts in the last 30 days (count, with reading date).
3. **Pillars**: 3-5 recurring themes (product, behind the scenes, customers, education, offers, team).
4. **Formats**: reels, carousels, photos, story highlights, long videos, lives; real photos or graphics; faces.
5. **Visual style**: colours, fonts, logo, consistency across channels and with site and ads.
6. **Tone and copy**: who speaks (company or person), length, hooks, CTA, hashtags.
7. **Top posts**: the 5-10 with most engagement or views (visible numbers, date). What they have in common.
8. **Engagement**: real or empty comments, does the owner reply? how fast? handling of criticism.
9. **Bio and path**: link in bio (where it goes), contact buttons, highlights (offers, reviews, FAQ).
10. **Organic social proof**: customer content, shared reviews, collaborations, "Paid partnership" (listed in the Meta Ad Library).
11. **Google Business Profile**: posts, photos, Q&A, hours, categories.
12. **Email and owned content**: newsletter, lead magnet, blog/SEO (subscribe only with user approval).

## Organic vs paid (the most useful part)
- Which organic posts become ads (same content in the Ad Library) = what they know works.
- Ad-only posts not visible on the profile = showcase and sales kept separate.
- One consistent message or two different voices? A profile dead for months with active ads is a weak point (low credibility).

## Rules
- Manual reading of public profiles, no automated collection (Meta terms).
- Analyse the company's content, not the commenters (no names in reports).
- Followers, likes and views are point-in-time: always date and source. "Big following" does not mean "sells": organic -> sales link = INFERRED.
