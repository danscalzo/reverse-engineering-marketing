# Reading and verdict (after the facts, never before)

## One move on top (before anything else in the answer and in the report)
Three lines, written last, placed first:
- **Constraint**: the one thing that limits results most (tracking, offer, traffic, landing, follow-up). One, not three.
- **Move**: the single action that attacks it. Default order: do MORE of what already works, then do it BETTER, only then something NEW.
- **Check**: the number to watch, the threshold and the date ("if cost per purchase is not under X by <date>, the move was wrong").
If the evidence does not support one move (outside-in with thin data), say "no single move established" and name what would establish it. Never pad it with a list.

## Business lens
- **Market**: growing? pain with a trigger event? money available?
- **Real buyer**: who decides and pays (e.g. the parent who pays, not the student who uses it).
- **Economics**: the real question is cost per SALE, not per lead. Needed: % qualified, % closed, margin. If missing: UNKNOWN, plus what would resolve it (account access, an interview, a mystery-shop test only with user approval).
- **Constraint**: leads, sales or delivery? Name one.
- **Offer value** (Hormozi's value equation, public framework): dream outcome, perceived likelihood (proof), time delay, effort. Which of the four does their offer push?
- **More / Better / New**: what they would do more of, better, or new.

## Best and worst
- 3 winners and 3 losers with numbers and source.
- The why as a declared HYPOTHESIS. Losers with tiny spend are noise. High CTR does not mean leads.

## Verdict
- Does it work? On what (volume, cost, quality), and what is not known. "Not established" is stated plainly.
- What to copy (structure, logic), what to improve, what NOT to copy.
- Keep what you observed apart from what you propose.
- How it could be automated, and what the business would need to provide.
- Who else it fits, and how it compares with whoever already sells it.
- One experiment to test the most important hypothesis (A/B with a baseline).

## Experiment card (one per strongest hypothesis)
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis | "If we do X like the subject, Y improves because Z" |
| Baseline | current numbers, source, period |
| Change | one variable only |
| Measure | metric, threshold that means win / lose |
| Check dates | +7 and +14 days |
| Risk | budget, brand, compliance |
Launch only with the user's approval; log it wherever the user tracks experiments.

## Client-ready version
The report is the working file. If it must be shown to a client or prospect, turn it into a short deck or one-pager with the user's presentation skill or template: findings in plain language, 3 opportunities, 1 experiment, no internal ledger. Keep observed and proposed visibly apart.
